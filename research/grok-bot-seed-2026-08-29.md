# Grok Bot seed research

Reviewed: 29 August 2026 (UTC)

This is the source pack behind BotMRR's initial Grok Bot field notes. It is a discovery set, not an earnings leaderboard. Every entry links to the original public post. Self-reported workflows remain self-reported; public screenshots and creator-run dashboards are described only for what they actually show.

## What is officially live

- [Introducing Grok Bot](https://x.ai/news/introducing-grok-bot) confirms cloud computers, background work, routines, and multi-Bot collaboration.
- [Template sharing announcement](https://x.com/bot/status/2093376523919323618) confirms that users can publish share links and other users can add a copy.
- [Official Bot sharing documentation](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/bots) explains that a shared Bot exposes its identity, description, skills, and routines; the recipient gets a copy rather than the publisher's computer, account access, or history.
- [Grok Bot expands to more plans](https://x.ai/news/grok-bot-more-plans) materially increases the likely audience for templates and outcomes.
- [Third-party Bot terms](https://x.ai/legal/bot-sharing-terms) restrict redistributing or re-exporting a Bot configuration without the publisher's permission. BotMRR should link to the xAI page, credit the named publisher, and never mirror the configuration by default.

I found official template sharing, but no official xAI directory announcement in the sources reviewed. BotMRR should not build its strategy around a directory launch that has not been publicly documented.

- Source: [nyk, 22 Aug](https://x.com/nykdotdev/status/2091068926235144607)

### 7. Seven-Bot content team

- Source: [Ridark, 25 Aug](https://x.com/ridark_eth/status/2092292504066429381)
- Use on BotMRR: content-operations case study.
- Why it matters: the system defines discovery, research, writing, visual, distribution, analytics, and chief-of-staff roles around one calendar. The post reports 14 of 25 drafts shipped in week one.
- Caveat: the production figures are self-reported and should not become performance benchmarks without a work log.

### 8. Newsletter unsubscribe audit

- Source: [Shimecki, 26 Aug](https://x.com/scheemunai/status/2092591711557947796)
- Use on BotMRR: bounded inbox workflow.
- Why it matters: the Bot first creates a review page with reasoning, waits for a human choice, then carries out approved unsubscribes and logs progress.
- Caveat: the reported 76 unsubscribes have not been independently tested; the valuable part is the approval pattern.

### 9. One-person newsletter business

- Source: [Greg Isenberg, 21 Aug](https://x.com/gregisenberg/status/2090901863814017300)
- Use on BotMRR: one-person-company guide.
- Why it matters: it frames research, drafting, editing, scheduling, and growth as separate Bot roles that one non-technical operator can supervise.
- Caveat: this is a second-hand profile and includes no revenue evidence.

### 10. Calendar history to relationship CRM

- Source: [Gaurav Munjal, 28 Aug](https://x.com/gauravmunjal/status/2093366092920144201)
- Use on BotMRR: back-office case study.
- Why it matters: the post describes turning ten years of calendar history into an enriched Notion relationship database; the first three years reportedly produced about 1,000 contacts in 2.5 hours.
- Caveat: it was an in-progress report, and the accuracy of enrichment was not audited.

### 11. DeckLens pitch-deck analyzer

- Source: [Brian D. Evans, 28 Aug](https://x.com/BrianDEvans/status/2093386515972030780)
- Template reply: [Brian's direct links](https://x.com/BrianDEvans/status/2093386518375346484)
- Template: [DeckLens on xAI](https://x.ai/bot/KlcxAG1I8cMQoqS_8Hrdn)
- Use on BotMRR: shareable template and investing workflow.
- Why it matters: it encodes one investor's screening criteria so incoming decks can be scored before manual review.
- Caveat: the public template proves shareability, not the quality or fairness of its scoring.

### 12. Email prompt-injection boundaries

- Source: [Peter, 25 Aug](https://x.com/aye_pete/status/2092324168037707992)
- Use on BotMRR: safety guide.
- Why it matters: the prompt treats subject lines, bodies, senders, attachments, links, and hidden text as untrusted data and requires confirmation before consequential actions.
- Caveat: prompt text is defense in depth, not a hard security boundary. Email Bots still need narrow permissions, action gates, and audit logs.

### 13. VISION.md-driven open-source triage

- Source: [Kun Chen, 23 Aug](https://x.com/kunchenguid/status/2091638832307536357)
- Use on BotMRR: open-source operations case.
- Why it matters: a written project vision becomes the decision layer for Bot-assisted issue and pull-request triage, moving the maintainer's attention from individual tickets toward direction.
- Caveat: the triage quality and causal effect on maintainer workload remain self-reported.

### 14. The $10,000 sponsorship claim

- Source: [Alex Finn, 21 Aug](https://x.com/AlexFinn/status/2090939211159650633)
- Use on BotMRR: proof-request case, never a verified revenue card.
- What the public artifact supports: an outbound email quoting a $10,000 sponsorship rate.
- What it does not support: acceptance, a signed contract, invoice, settlement, collected cash, net revenue, or Bot attribution.

### 15. Recovered cash, credit, and subscriptions

- Source: [Royce, 21 Aug](https://x.com/royce_james/status/2090617019708408140)
- Use on BotMRR: economic-value proof request.
- What is claimed: $300 in unclaimed funds, a $150 flight credit, and $50 per month in subscriptions identified for cancellation.
- What remains missing: receipts, redemption or deposit confirmation, cancellation confirmation, and a savings baseline. These are three different value categories and none is revenue.

## Product conclusion

BotMRR should ship two visibly separate surfaces:

1. **Field notes** for useful public workflows, templates, safety patterns, and ecosystem news.
2. **Claim reviews and future receipts** for economic outcomes, with money evidence, Bot control, and attribution kept as separate checks.

The durable positioning is:

> Directories show what Bots exist. BotMRR shows what the evidence actually supports.

No current seed should receive a generic “verified” check. The first real connected receipt should start with a narrow revenue metric—gross sales, net collected, payout earnings, or MRR—then show Bot control and attribution as independent labels.
